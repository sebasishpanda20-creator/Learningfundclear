Strategy notebook

Review notes from your uploaded guides. These are research candidates, not new automatic Buy/Sell signals. Record exact rules, costs and failed setups, then test on unseen data before considering live use.

Intraday: opening-range breakout

Choose a 15- or 30-minute opening range before the session. Wait for it to finish, then evaluate a completed candle outside its high or low. Define direct-breakout versus retest entry separately, an invalidation level and a last-entry time. Skip incomplete session history.

Use the actual instrument's exchange session and holiday calendar. A 24-hour crypto market has no single equity-style opening bell. Source: Opening Range Breakout guide, pp. 2–8.

Swing: trend pullback and breakout/retest

Separate the higher-timeframe trend from the entry timeframe. Record the prior level, pullback, completed confirmation candle and invalidation. Compare volume with that instrument's own history when reliable volume exists. Do not substitute index spot volume with zero or assume crypto volume is global.

Sources: Swing Trading Strategies guide, pp. 5–8; Trend Trading Strategies guide, pp. 5–8; Volume guide, pp. 7–9.

Market cycle: EMA crossback and base breakout

Track whether price is reclaiming averages, making its first pullback, consolidating, or extending away from them. Review relative strength against a suitable benchmark. Define “tight base” and “extended” numerically before automated testing.

The Oliver Kell workbook uses 10/20 EMA context and separates weekly context, daily setups and lower-timeframe execution. It is not the same rule as the existing portal's 20/50 trend filter. Source: workbook pp. 3–7; slides p. 8. The bullish Base n’ Break slide repeats Crossback text; the workbook provides the distinct explanation.

Supply/demand, order blocks and candle patterns

Record zone creation time, boundaries, retests and invalidation. A chart zone is a price-derived hypothesis; candles do not prove institutional orders. Pin bars, divergence, Fibonacci levels and gaps need explicit confirmation rules. Gaps need not fill, and divergence can persist.

Sources: Supply and Demand guide pp. 6–8; Order Block guide pp. 5–6; Pin Bar guide pp. 4–9; Divergence guide pp. 5 and 13; Gap guide p. 4. No claimed success rate has been adopted.

Keep exit methods separate

Your materials disagree about moving stops to entry, trailing stops and partial exits. Keep each as a named test variant with identical entries and realistic costs. Entry-price stops do not eliminate fees, slippage or gap risk. Do not tighten a stop solely to manufacture an attractive reward/risk number.

Sources: Trend Following guide p. 29; Pip Factory p. 29; Swing Trading beginner guide pp. 81–83. The paper ledger records manual full-position exits; an exit-method note is not an automated order.

What needs more data before automation?

Order flow requires an appropriate trades/order-book feed. Options require fresh bid/ask quotes and contract details. Deep-value screening requires point-in-time financials. Historical foreign exchange rules, broker recommendations and US dollar tick offsets do not transfer directly to Indian instruments. FYERS remains deferred.

Testing checklist
  • Freeze entry, stop, target, timeframe and exit rules before testing.
  • Use only candles and confirmed pivots available at the decision time.
  • Include fees, spreads, slippage, missed fills and gap-through-stop losses.
  • Keep development data separate from an untouched test period.
  • Report trade count, net expectancy, drawdown and losing streaks, including no-trade periods.
  • Paper-test forward. A high reward/risk ratio or selected winning charts is not evidence of an edge.
